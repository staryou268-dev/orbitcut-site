Press, point, release.

There is nothing to learn. If you can move a mouse, you already know how to use it.

  1. Press your shortcut

    The menu appears under your cursor. The default is Control, Option, P, and you can change it to any combination you like.

  2. Point at a slice

    Each app has its own menu. Hover over a slice and the name and keyboard shortcut show in the centre.

  3. Let go, or click

    Hold the shortcut and release over a slice, or tap it once to keep the menu open and click. Press 1 to 9 to pick by number.

Built to stay out of your way.

OrbitCut lives in your menu bar, opens in a fraction of a second, and does nothing until you call it.

Menus that build themselves

Open an app OrbitCut has not seen before and it reads that app's menu bar, then starts with the three commands people reach for most. Add an app in Settings and it starts with eight.

It learns your habits

Command shortcuts you use often earn a slice of their own. OrbitCut counts only the shortcut and the app, never what you type, and the counts stay on your Mac. You can switch it off or wipe it at any time.

A separate menu for the desktop

Point at the desktop instead of a window and you get a different menu: new folder, screenshot, wallpaper, Spotlight and more.

Questions

Which Macs does it run on?

OrbitCut needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later. The Liquid Glass look needs macOS 26, and earlier versions use the frosted style automatically.

Why does it ask for Accessibility access?

macOS requires it for any app that presses keyboard shortcuts for you, notices your OrbitCut shortcut, or reads another app's menu names. OrbitCut does not read your documents, and nothing it sees leaves your Mac. macOS asks once, and you can turn it off at any time in System Settings.

What data do you collect?

None about how you use your Mac. The app only contacts the internet to check your licence key and to look for updates. Read the privacy policy for the details.

Does it work with every app?

It works with any app that shows keyboard shortcuts in its menu bar, and you can add your own slices to any app. A few apps, such as some games and cross-platform tools, hide their menus. For those, OrbitCut shows a general menu that you can edit.

Can I try it before I buy?

Yes. Download it, and every feature is free for 7 days. After that you keep 10 menu opens a day without paying anything.

How many Macs can I use it on?

One licence covers up to 3 of your Macs. To move it to a new Mac, deactivate the old one from the app's settings.

Can I open it with a mouse button?

Yes. Use your mouse's own software to map a button to your OrbitCut shortcut.
